Image 1 is the Sea Swings, a swing ride in our painted storybook map of a seaside amusement park; image 2 is our style frame. Draw a sprite sheet of the SWING CHAIRS that hang from that ride's canopy on chains, in exactly the painting's style: chunky, rounded, toy-like proportions, thin warm dark-brown outlines (#2c201b) around every silhouette, thinner tinted lines inside, soft pastel fills with one soft shade tone, a gentle paper texture. Not cleaner, not brighter, not more saturated than image 1.

Each chair is a classic wave-swinger seat: a moulded seat with a rounded backrest, painted in one colour with a thin gold trim, two slim metal side frames that rise from the seat's sides to a small ring at the top of each side (where a chain will hook on), a thin safety bar across the front, and a small footrest bar below the front edge. The chairs are EMPTY: no people, no animals. There are NO chains: just the chair.

Layout, strict: a grid of 5 columns and 6 rows on a flat, pure magenta background (#FF00FF) with nothing else on it. Every chair is whole, centred in its cell with a wide empty margin; nothing touches or overlaps a neighbour. All chairs are drawn at the same scale. No shadows, no ground, no grid lines, no borders, no text.

Each ROW is one chair, the same chair in all five cells, identical in colour and decoration:
Row 1: a single seat, butter yellow.
Row 2: a single seat, mint green.
Row 3: a single seat, coral red.
Row 4: a single seat, sky blue.
Row 5: a double seat (one bench for two, twice as wide as a single), orange.
Row 6: a double seat (one bench for two, twice as wide as a single), pink.

Each COLUMN is one viewing direction, the same in every row, all seen from slightly above (about 25 degrees down, as the ride in image 1 is seen):
Column 1: side view, the chair facing RIGHT, in profile (backrest on the left, the front of the seat on the right).
Column 2: three-quarter view, the chair facing to the right and towards the viewer.
Column 3: front view, the chair facing straight towards the viewer (we see the front of the seat and the backrest behind it).
Column 4: three-quarter view, the chair facing to the right and away from the viewer (we see the back of the backrest and its right side).
Column 5: back view, the chair facing straight away from the viewer (we see the back of the backrest).
