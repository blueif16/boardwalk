The whole park on one screen

park.html: the whole park fixed to fill any screen, no scrolling, no bars. The painting is 3:2 with town and hills above and the beach and sea below, so on 16:10 (Mac) and 16:9 (monitor) screens only those margins are trimmed and the park stays whole. The buttons put animals on it at 30 / 38 / 47 / 60 / 70 (a static scale preview) and print how many pixels tall an animal is on your screen.

Why the whole park looks soft, and the fix

Every image was made at the top settings. The softness comes from how they were chained: adding the town and sea repainted the whole picture and lost most of the park's fine detail, and one image has to hold the whole park. Redrawing it in pieces at full 4K, then shrinking to the screen, fixes it.

Now: the 4K stage, and how big the animals should be

The stage is redrawn at 3840 × 2560 (gpt-image-2.5, quality high), 2.5× the old one, with the ghost figures gone. It lines up with the old stage to under a pixel (median), so every measured position still holds. Full 4K stage · live page.

Character size: 30, 38 or 47

The number is how tall a standing grown-up animal is, ears to feet, in stage pixels (the stage is 1536 × 1024 of them). Kids and pets are smaller. In the scene:

  • Lamp post about 105 tall, stall about 120 roof to floor, rail about 18, platform fence 20–27.
  • 30 (the brief): an animal is under a third of a lamp post, under twice the rail. Room to move; reads as a crowd.
  • 38: about a third of a lamp post, twice the rail. In between.
  • 47 (what the style frame drew): about half a lamp post, two and a half rails. Chunkier and closer to the style frame, but queues swallow the platform gates and the deck fills up.
  • On screen: the page fits the stage's height to the window, so on a 1080p monitor one stage pixel is about one screen pixel: 30 → about 30 px tall, 47 → about 47 px.
